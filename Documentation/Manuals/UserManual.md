# Cosmic Daybook — User Guide for Teachers

**A complete guide to managing your Montessori classroom**

---

# Getting Started

## First Launch

When you open Cosmic Daybook for the first time, you'll see a four-page welcome walkthrough:

1. **Welcome** — An overview of what the app does: manage students, lessons, work, and observations all in one place.
2. **Add Your Students** — A reminder to start by adding your students. You can organize them by level and track their lesson history.
3. **Plan Your Lessons** — An introduction to the lesson library and the work lifecycle (active, review, complete).
4. **Quick Notes & More** — How to use the floating button for fast access to notes, presentations, work items, and to-dos.

Tap **Next** to move through each page, or tap **Skip** to jump straight into the app. On the last page, tap **Get Started** to begin.

## The Main Screen

After setup, you'll land on the **Today** view — your daily dashboard. On iPhone the bar along the bottom holds the four places you go every day, and **More** holds everything else; on iPad and Mac the same groups appear in the sidebar:

| Tab | What it does |
|-----|-------------|
| Today | Your daily hub — lessons, work, reminders, attendance |
| Students | Your student roster and profiles |
| Attendance | Mark students present, tardy, or absent |
| Lessons & Work | Plan upcoming presentations, follow what needs attention, review children’s active work, and browse presentations and completed work |
| More | Everything else, in groups |

The groups under **More** (and in the sidebar) are:

| Group | What it holds |
|-------|---------------|
| Today | Today, Attendance, Todos |
| Children | Students, Meetings, Notes (your observations), Parent Reports |
| Plan | Lessons & Work, Groups, Class Grid, Calendar, This Week’s Parsha |
| Classroom | Restock, Community, Clubs & Projects, Going Out, Schedules |
| Library | Lessons (your full lesson library), Albums, Resources |
| System | Settings |

On the Mac, click a group's heading to collapse or expand it; the app remembers your choice. **Classroom** starts collapsed because you reach for it a few times a term. Choosing something inside a collapsed group — with a keyboard shortcut, say — opens the group for you.

Some screens you may remember now live inside others, and old links and saved windows take you to the right place:

| If you are looking for… | Find it in… |
|-------------------------|-------------|
| Logs | **Notes** for observations; **History** or **List** inside Meetings, Attendance and Lessons & Work for the rest |
| Three-Year View, Progress Dashboard, Lesson Recall | **Class Grid**, on that view |
| Parsha Calendar | **This Week’s Parsha**, with the year list beside it (the **Year** button on iPhone) |
| Stories, Procedures | **Resources**, on that type |
| Book Club | **Clubs & Projects** |

The in-app Ask AI chat has been removed.

## Switching Between My Class and Sample Class

Use the **My Class** control in the Mac toolbar or the classroom controls at the top of iPhone and iPad to change classrooms.

- **My Class** contains your real classroom records and continues to use your normal iCloud and sharing setup.
- **Sample Class** is a local practice classroom with fake students, attendance, observations, and lesson activity. It has its own database and never changes records in My Class.
- The lesson catalog is copied from My Class when you enter Sample Class, so lesson names, write-ups, attachments, and follow-up work are available with the fake roster.
- A blue banner remains visible whenever Sample Class is active. Use **Return to My Class** in that banner when you are finished practicing.

Backup and restore commands are unavailable while Sample Class is active, which prevents a practice data set from being mistaken for a real classroom backup.

## The Floating Button

In the bottom-right corner of every screen, you'll see a small glass-style button. This is your **quick action hub** — the fastest way to do almost anything in the app.

| Gesture | What happens |
|---------|-------------|
| Double-tap | Opens the Command Bar (type a command in plain English) |
| Long-press (hold for about half a second) | Opens the Pie Menu with five quick actions |
| Drag | Moves the button to a new position on screen (it stays where you put it) |

### The Pie Menu

When you long-press the floating button, five colored segments fan out in a circle:

| Segment | Color | What it does |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| Present | Blue | Record or schedule a lesson presentation |
| Work | Orange | Create a new work item |
| Practice | Pink | Log a practice session |
| Todo | Green | Add a to-do for yourself |
| Note | Purple | Write a quick observation |

Slide your finger to the action you want and release.

### The Command Bar

Double-tap the floating button to open a text field where you can type commands in plain English:

- "Gave Sophia long division" — records a presentation
- "Note about Emma's counting — struggling with teen numbers" — creates an observation note
- "Todo: buy more bead bars by Friday" — creates a to-do with a due date
- "Assign counting work to Leo" — creates a work item

The app parses your words, identifies students and lessons by name, and fills in the details. You'll see a confirmation before anything is saved.

# Today View

The Today view is your daily command center. Everything you need for the current day is here.

## Navigating Dates

At the top, you'll see today's date with arrow buttons on either side. Tap the arrows to move forward or back one school day (weekends and holidays you've marked as non-school days are automatically skipped).

## Sections

The Today view is organized into collapsible sections. From top to bottom:

### Attendance Strip

A quick row of student indicators showing who's present, absent, or not yet marked. Tap to expand the full attendance grid.

### Reminders

Any reminders due today or overdue from previous days.

### Lessons Due

Presentations you've scheduled for today. Tap a lesson to open it, view the lesson plan, and mark it as given.

### Meetings

Student meetings scheduled for today. Tap to open the meeting and record notes.

### Scheduled Check-Ins

Work items with check-ins due today. Tap to log your observation.

### Schedule

If you have set up a schedule in **Schedules**, today's slots appear here, grouped by schedule. The section stays out of the way when there is no schedule.

### Todos

Your to-do items for today. Tap the checkbox to mark them complete.

### Today's Pad

The pad is the day's note — a place to jot whatever you want to remember about the day. It is the one note for a day: the **Calendar** reads and writes the same pad, so a note you add on a Calendar day shows up on that day's pad and the other way around. Notes you wrote on the Calendar before this change were copied into their days' pads for you (on the Mac, at launch); until a note has been copied, iPhone and iPad show it beside the pad, read only.

### Completed Items

A collapsed section at the bottom showing everything you've finished today — completed work, given lessons, and finished meetings. Useful for reviewing your day.

# Students

## Viewing Your Roster

Tap **Students** in the navigation to see your class, grouped by level (Lower Elementary, Upper Elementary, Adolescent). Every row reads the same way whatever the order:

- **Name**, with **Absent** or **Left Early** beside it when today's attendance says so, and **Birthday today** or **Birthday Tue** when a birthday falls in the coming week. A green dot on the picture means the child is here.
- **Lesson … days ago**: school days since the child's last lesson. It turns orange at 7 school days, the same rule Today uses for "need a lesson".
- **Observed …**: when you last wrote an observation about the child. It turns orange after two weeks.

The chips above the list narrow it, and each shows how many children it holds: **All**, **Here**, **Due** (due for a lesson), and one per level that has children this year. Until attendance is taken on a school day, a line under the chips says so.

The sort button names the active order: **A–Z**, **Manual**, **Age** (each row adds the child's age), or **Next Birthday** (each row adds the date). Use the search bar to find a student quickly.

On iPhone, swipe a row left to **Observe** (a quick note for that child) or start a **Lesson** with the child already on it. On iPad and Mac the same actions are in the row's context menu.

On iPad and Mac, when no child is open, the right side shows **Class at a Glance**: who's absent, who's due for a lesson, who hasn't been observed lately, and whose birthday or Hebrew birthday is this week. Tap any name to open the record. On the Mac the roster is a table: the selected child stays highlighted, the arrow keys move through the class, every column sorts, and **Age**, **Birthday** and **Hebrew Birthday** columns can be shown from the column header's menu. **Former** above the table lists students who have left.

## Adding a Student

Tap the **Add Student** button (or use the add icon). Fill in:

- **First Name** (required)
- **Last Name** (required)
- **Nickname** (optional — used for display if set)
- **Birthday** (date picker)
- **Born after sunset** — turn this on for a child born in the evening, since a Hebrew date begins at sunset
- **Start Date** (when the student joined your class)
- **Level** — Lower, Upper, or Adolescent

Tap **Add** and the student appears in your roster immediately.

## School Year Rollover

When a new school year begins and your class composition changes, use the guided rollover instead of editing students one at a time. Open it from the **Add Student** menu in the Students toolbar (**School Year Rollover…**), or from Settings → General → School Calendar.

The flow has three steps:

1. **Assign** — For each student choose an outcome: **Stay**, **Promote** (to the next level — Lower → Upper → Adolescent), **Transfer Out** (moved to another class at your school), or **Withdraw** (left the school). Use the **Set All** menu on a level header to assign a whole group at once. The effective date defaults to the last day of the outgoing school year, which keeps departing students visible in that year's view and out of the new year's roster. Leave "Log an observation for each change" on to record each move in the student's notes timeline.
2. **Review** — Check the summary ("7 promoted to Adolescent · 12 transferred · …") and tap **Apply Rollover**. All changes are saved together.
3. **Done** — For each departing student, tap **Generate Summary** to produce a PDF report to hand to their next guide. Reports draw from observations flagged "Include in report" (the rollover's own observation is flagged automatically).

Nothing is deleted by a rollover:

- **Promoted** students stay on your active roster at their new level, and their profile shows the move (for example "Promoted · Upper → Adolescent").
- **Transferred** and **Withdrawn** students move to the collapsed **Former Students** section at the bottom of the roster, labeled with how they left. Their full history — observations, presentations, attendance, work — is preserved, they still appear in search, and past-year reports and logs continue to include them. Re-enroll a former student at any time by editing them and setting Enrollment back to Enrolled.

## Student Detail View

Tap any student to open their full profile. You'll see several tabs:

Under **Birthday**, the profile shows the child's **Hebrew birthday** in English and in Hebrew, worked out from the date of birth; you never type it. If the child was born after sunset, edit the student and turn on **Born after sunset**, and the Hebrew birthday moves to the next day's date.

### Progress Tab

An overview of where this student stands across the curriculum. Shows lesson completion by subject and group, with visual indicators for active work, items in review, and mastered lessons.

### Meetings Tab

All one-on-one meetings with this student. You can schedule new meetings, view past meeting notes, and track outcomes.

### Notes Tab

A timeline of every observation you've recorded for this student. Filter by category (Academic, Behavioral, Social, Emotional, Health, General) or search by text. Newest notes appear first.

### Files Tab

Documents attached to this student's profile — report cards, assessments, photos of work samples. Tap to view or add new files.

# Lessons

## The Lesson Library

Tap **Lessons** to browse your curriculum. Lessons are organized hierarchically:

- **Subjects** (e.g., Math, Language, Science, Practical Life, Sensorial)
  - **Groups** within each subject (e.g., "Counting," "Phonics," "Botany")
    - **Individual lessons** in each group

You can search by lesson name, filter by subject, or scroll through the full library.

## Lesson Detail

Tap any lesson to see its full details:

| Section | What it shows |
|---------|-------------|
| Name & Subject | Title, subject, and group |
| Write-Up | The full lesson plan and presentation script |
| Materials | What you need to have ready |
| Purpose | The educational objective |
| Age Range | Recommended student ages |
| Teacher Notes | Your personal notes about teaching this lesson |
| Follow-Up Work | Suggested activities for after the presentation |
| Files | The lesson's attachments (PDFs, images, Pages documents) and any Resources linked to it, together in one list; tap a Resource to open it |
| Lesson Journey | A timeline showing which students have received this lesson and their progress |

## Adding a Lesson

Tap the add button from the Lessons screen. Fill in:

- **Name** (required)
- **Subject** (required — choose from existing subjects or create a new one)
- **Group** (required — choose from existing groups or create a new one)
- **Format** — Standard (a regular lesson) or Story (a lesson with parent-child branching for narrative-based curriculum)
- **Source** — Album (from your Montessori album), Personal (your own lesson), or Great Lesson
- **Materials, Purpose, Age Range, Teacher Notes, Suggested Follow-Up Work** (all optional but recommended)

You can also **import lessons from a CSV file** for bulk entry — useful when setting up your library for the first time.

## Story-Format Lessons

If you choose the Story format, you can create branching lessons: a parent story with child chapters that extend it. This is useful for cosmic education and narrative-based Montessori curriculum where one Great Lesson branches into many follow-up explorations.

# Lessons & Work

**Lessons & Work** keeps the whole presentation-to-practice cycle in one place. A **presentation** is the act of giving a lesson to one or more children; work is the independent activity that follows. The workspace helps you prepare, observe, and decide what comes next without maintaining separate presentation and work planners.

## Workspace Views

| View | What it shows |
|------|---------------|
| Needs Attention | Open presentation follow-ups and work that is due, overdue, ready for review, or has gone too long without a check-in |
| Upcoming | Presentations that are ready or scheduled, plus children who may need a lesson |
| Children Working | All active child work, with search, work-type filters, sorting, scheduling, and completion actions |
| History | Recorded presentations and completed child work. Each half (presentations and work) has its own **History** choice; finished work is under History |

On Mac, choose these views from the segmented control in the toolbar. On iPhone, choose them from the **Lessons & Work** view menu. Search follows the selected view.

## Shared Agenda

The **Agenda** shows scheduled presentations and work check-ins together across upcoming school days. On Mac, it is a resizable lower pane that you can show or hide. On iPhone, it starts hidden; tap the calendar button to swap the current view for the Agenda. The Agenda is available in **Needs Attention**, **Upcoming**, and **Children Working**, but not **History**.

## Recording a Presentation

There are several ways to record a presentation:

1. **From the Pie Menu** — Long-press the floating button, select the blue "Present" segment
2. **From a Lesson** — Open a lesson's detail view and tap "Give Lesson" or "Assign"
3. **From the Today View** — Tap a scheduled lesson to open and record it
4. **From the Command Bar** — Type something like "Gave Emma long division"

## Recording It and What Comes Next

Open a planned presentation and the sheet asks **Who was there?** Each child is ticked from today's attendance; a child marked absent starts unticked and stays on the plan for next time. Tap a child to change it, choose the day if it wasn't today, and press **Record Presentation** (⌘↩ on the Mac).

The same sheet then turns into **How it went**:

- **What did you notice?** One note for the group, typed or dictated. **Split by Child** files the parts about each child on that child's row.
- **What's next for each child**: Practice, Follow-up work, Re-present, Ready for next, or Keep watching. Set it once on the **Everyone** row, then tap only the children who differ. Choose when to check the work if you want a date.
- **When you press Done** says exactly what will be created. **Done** saves it all; **Later** saves your notes and keeps the decisions waiting in **Following Presentations**. **Undo** in the header takes the recording back.

For the everyday case, use **Presented Today** on a Ready lesson in **Lessons & Work** (the button appears on hover on the Mac, or long-press the row). It records the lesson for the children who are here and adds their usual next step; the message that follows has **Undo** and **Details…**.

## The Presentation Form

When you create a presentation, you'll fill in:

### Lesson

Choose which lesson you're presenting. You can search by name or browse by subject.

### Students

Select one or more students who will receive this lesson. You can search by name and select multiple students.

### Date

By default, the date is set to today. You can:

- **Mark as Given** — Record it as already presented (today or a past date)
- **Schedule for Later** — Set a future date when you plan to give the lesson

### Notes

Free-form text for recording how the presentation went — what the student responded to, what was difficult, any observations.

### Follow-Up Options

Each child begins at **Keep Watching**, which creates no deadline or extra work. You can leave that in place or choose:

- **Check Work** — Return during a later work cycle, on the next school day, or on a date you choose
- **Plan Support** — Re-present, offer a follow-up presentation, or confer with the child
- **Plan Next Presentation** — Review and explicitly add or schedule a related or next lesson
- **Child’s Follow-Up Work** — Record the exact work invited for one child or the group

Unresolved choices appear in **Needs Attention**. Work appears in **Children Working**, and a dated work check-in also appears in the shared **Agenda**. Adding work never chooses the guide’s follow-up automatically.

## Presentation States

Each presentation moves through a lifecycle:

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Draft | Created but not yet scheduled or given |
| Scheduled | Assigned to a specific future date |
| Presented | Lesson has been given — this becomes a permanent historical record |

Once a presentation is marked as "Presented," the lesson title and details are frozen as a snapshot. Even if you later edit the lesson, the historical record of what you presented remains unchanged.

# Work

**Work** represents the independent practice and follow-up activities students do after receiving a lesson. Work items track a student's progress from initial assignment through mastery.

## How Work Gets Created

Work items are typically created:

- **After a presentation** — When you record the exact work invited in **Child’s Follow-Up Work**
- **Manually** — From the Pie Menu (orange "Work" segment) or the Command Bar
- **From Lessons & Work** — When you choose **New Work** in **Children Working**

## Work Detail

Each work item shows:

- **Title** — The name of the work (often matching the lesson name)
- **Student(s)** — Who is assigned
- **Lesson** — The lesson this work relates to
- **Status** — Working, Needs Review, Mastered, Finished, Keep Practicing, or Incomplete
- **Due Date** — When you expect the work to be finished (optional)
- **Steps** — A breakdown of the work into individual steps (optional)
- **Check-Ins** — Your observations recorded during the work period
- **Notes** — Any observations attached to this work item

## The Work Lifecycle

A work item has one status, and it is per child. Two statuses keep the work open; four close it.

| Status | What it means |
|--------|-------------|
| Working | The child is working on it |
| Needs Review | She may be ready — you need to look before it moves on |
| Mastered | Closed: she has it. This is the practice evidence the mastery candidates read |
| Finished | Closed: the work is done, and mastery doesn't apply to it. It never counts as mastery |
| Keep Practicing | Closed: this invitation is done, but she is not there yet |
| Incomplete | Closed: set aside unfinished |

Work closed before September 2026 with no verdict shows as **Finished** too, since both mean closed with no verdict.

Closing a work logs it, like recording a presentation: the completion is recorded for the child, that day's check-in is marked done, any later check-ins are skipped, and the work leaves the Scheduled strip. Changing the status back to Working or Needs Review reopens it.

### Logging a check

- **Right-click a work** — on a pill in the Scheduled strip or on a card in Lessons & Work — and pick a status under **Log as**. When several children share the work, the menu offers **Everyone** and then one submenu per child, so one right-click can say "all three mastered it, but Naomi needs another go".
- **Click a pill in the Scheduled strip** to open the log sheet: one row per child with her status and a note. Nothing is written until you press **Log Check**. A child you leave untouched still has her check-in marked done, because the check happened.
- Every log can be undone from the toast for a few seconds.

## Check-Ins

A check-in is a quick observation you record while a student is working. To log one:

1. Open the work item
2. Tap "Add Check-In"
3. Write your observation (what you saw, how the student is progressing)
4. Optionally rate the quality
5. Optionally schedule the next check-in

Check-ins build a timeline of progress for each work item.

## Practice Sessions

A **practice session** is a quick log of students practicing during work time. You can record:

- Which students were practicing
- Which work item(s) they were on
- Duration
- Whether it was individual or group practice
- Quality observations

Access this from the Pie Menu (pink "Practice" segment).

# Notes & Observations

Notes are the primary way to capture classroom observations. You can attach them to specific students, link them to lessons, and categorize them for later reference.

## Creating a Note

### From the Pie Menu

Long-press the floating button and select the purple "Note" segment. A quick note sheet opens with:

- **Student(s)** — Select one or more students (or "All Students" for a general classroom observation)
- **Body** — Your observation text
- **Tags** — Optional labels for categorization
- **Date** — Defaults to now, but you can change it
- **Photo** — Attach a picture (optional)

### From the Command Bar

Type something like:

- "Note about Emma — great focus during counting today"
- "Observation: Leo helped a younger student with practical life"

### From a Student's Profile

Open the student's detail view, go to the Notes tab, and tap the add button.

## Note Scope

Each note can be scoped to:

- **All students** — A general classroom observation
- **One student** — Specific to that child
- **Multiple students** — An observation about a group interaction

## Tags and Categories

Notes can be tagged with custom labels. Common categories include Academic, Behavioral, Social, Emotional, Health, and General. You can create your own tags in Settings.

## Viewing Notes

Notes appear in several places:

- **Student detail view** — Notes tab shows all observations for that student
- **Today view** — Recent notes from today
- **Notes** (from the navigation) — Opens your observations list directly. Search and filter across all notes

## Note Templates

In Settings, you can create templates — pre-written observation starters that save time during busy classroom moments. Templates can include default tags and category settings.

# Todos

Todos are your personal task list — things you need to do that aren't directly student work items.

## Creating a Todo

From the Pie Menu (green "Todo" segment) or the Command Bar:

- **Title** (required) — What you need to do
- **Description** (optional) — Additional details
- **Due Date** (optional) — When it's due
- **Recurrence** — None, Daily, Weekdays, Weekly, Biweekly, Monthly, Yearly, or Custom
- **Subtasks** — Break the todo into smaller steps

### Smart Parsing

The Command Bar understands natural language for todos:

- "Buy bead bars by Friday" — Creates a todo with title "Buy bead bars" and due date set to Friday
- "Finish report daily" — Creates a recurring daily todo
- "Remind me to call Emma's parents" — Creates a todo titled "Call Emma's parents"

## Todos in Today View

Your incomplete todos for today appear in a dedicated section. Tap the checkbox to mark them complete. Recurring todos automatically generate the next occurrence.

## Subtasks

Complex todos can have subtasks — a checklist within the todo. Check off each subtask as you complete it. The parent todo tracks how many subtasks are done.

## Sharing with Tide

If you also use the Tide app for your classroom to-dos, Cosmic Daybook can share its to-do list with it, so you see one list in either app.

- **Turn it on** in **Settings ▸ Connections ▸ Tide** on the Mac, with Tide open. Sharing runs from the Mac; to-dos made on iPhone or iPad reach Tide when the Mac is on. If Tide isn't open, the screen says so, and to-dos go across when it is.
- **What goes to Tide:** your to-dos, except the automatic work check-ins ("Check in with Maya on …"), which stay here.
- **Ticking off works both ways.** Tick a to-do in either app and the other follows.
- **From Tide:** to-dos that live in your Tide classroom lists show in a **From Tide** section in Todos. A Tide to-do that names a child (full name, or the short form such as "Maya S") also shows on that child's page. A first name two children share doesn't match.
- **Once a to-do has been sent, Tide owns the wording.** Changing its title here doesn't change it in Tide, so make wording changes in Tide.

The first time you turn sharing on, Cosmic Daybook matches to-dos you already have on both sides by their words, so you don't get doubles.

# Restock

Track your classroom materials and inventory so you always know what you have and what needs restocking. **Restock** is in the **Classroom** group.

## Supplies List

Tap **Restock** in the navigation to see all your tracked materials. Each supply shows:

- Name
- Status badge — **In Stock** (green), **Low** (yellow), **Critical** (orange), or **Out of Stock** (red)
- Category and location

## Adding a Supply

Tap the add button and fill in:

- **Name** (required) — e.g., "Colored Pencils"
- **Category** (required) — Materials, Books, Equipment, Consumables, Art Supplies, Food, or Other
- **Current Quantity** — How many you have now
- **Minimum Threshold** — The quantity at which the status switches to "Low"
- **Reorder Amount** — How many to order when restocking
- **Location** — Where it's stored in your classroom
- **Notes** — Any additional information

## Managing Inventory

From a supply's detail view, you can:

- **Adjust quantity** — Add or remove stock
- **Mark as on order** — Flag that you've ordered more
- **View transaction history** — See all additions and removals over time

The status automatically updates based on your current quantity versus the minimum threshold.

# Procedures

Document your classroom procedures and routines so you have them in one place — useful for substitutes, assistants, and your own reference. Procedures live in **Resources** (in the **Library** group) alongside Stories, Packets, and Files. Choose **Procedures** to see them, one type at a time.

## Adding a Procedure

Tap the add button with **Procedures** chosen in Resources:

- **Title** (required) — e.g., "Morning Arrival Routine"
- **Category** — Classroom Management, Practical Life, Transitions, Grace & Courtesy, Academic, or Other
- **Steps** — The step-by-step procedure
- **Materials** — What's needed
- **Age Group** — Which students this applies to
- **Notes** — Teaching tips or reminders

Procedures are searchable and filterable by category.

## Resources

**Resources** is the one place for your reference material. Choose a type: **Stories**, **Packets** (book club reading), **Files**, or **Procedures**. Only one type shows at a time, and the filter sits in the toolbar. Stories keep their cover pictures and story analysis; Files keep their categories, tags, favorites, and links to lessons and areas.

# Planning Tools

**Lessons & Work** is on the iPhone bar; the rest of the planning tools are under **More ▸ Plan** (or the Plan group in the sidebar).

## Lessons & Work

The shared planning workspace for presentations, follow-up, active child work, and history. Use **Needs Attention** for today’s observing and deciding, **Upcoming** to prepare presentations, **Children Working** to manage active work, and **History** to revisit presentations or completed work. The shared **Agenda** coordinates scheduled presentations and check-ins.

## Class Grid

The **Class Grid** is one screen for seeing the whole class against the curriculum, with four views. On the Mac, switch between them with the segmented control in the toolbar; on iPad and iPhone, use the tabs under the title.

| View | What it shows |
|------|---------------|
| Checklist | The class-wide checklist by area and group: which children have had which lessons. Check off items as you go — useful for weekly or monthly planning. |
| Three Years | Children grouped by year in class, with the Great Lessons first and then one row per area, showing each child's furthest mark. Pick an area to see its key lessons (or all of them). |
| Children | One row per child, grouped by level, with a strip for each sequence they have started (for example "4/8"). Click a strip to open that sequence: work, presentations, and notes. |
| Recall | A list rather than a grid: retention tiles (held, checked, to present again, faded over summer, due), a by-child list, and the queue of recall checks that are due, grouped by child, with the recall check beside it. |

The **area menu** ("All areas" or one area), **search**, and the **children filter** carry across all four views, so you can switch views without losing your place.

### Marks

The same marks are used everywhere in the grid:

- grey — no lesson given yet
- blue, filled a quarter, a half, or three-quarters — **Presented**, **Practicing**, **Reviewing**
- green with a check — **Mastered** (only when you have marked mastery)
- dashed orange — planned

Recall checks show as a ring around the mark: **solid green** still has it, **dashed orange** shaky, **dotted red** forgotten.

### Ready

Cells for lessons not yet given show whether the child is ready for them:

- **Ready** — a grey ring. The previous lesson is confirmed or mastered, no work from it is open, and the last check-in didn't ask for more practice.
- **Ready, check first** — a grey ring with an amber dot. The child is ready, but look first. Tap the cell and its card says why, for example "2 pieces of work still open".
- **Not yet** — a small grey dot.

The cell card also has a **Previous lesson** box: the lesson's name and the date it was given, any open work, the last check-in, and whether it was mastered. Confirming a lesson is not the same as mastery; only a mastery mark turns a cell green.

Groups and Today use the same two readiness levels, so they always agree with the grid.

### Narrowing the grid

The filter bar above the grid narrows what you see without changing any records:

- **Filter lessons** — type any part of a lesson, sequence, or section name and the rest of the rows hide. Several words all have to match, in any order, so "frac add" finds "Addition of Fractions". If nothing in the current area matches, the screen tells you which areas do and takes you there in one tap.
- **Students** — search and tick one or more children; every other column hides. Remove a name from the chips below the bar, or use **Show All** in the picker, to widen it back out.

Both filters can be on at once, and **Clear** drops them both. Cells you had selected for a batch action drop out of the selection when a filter hides them, so the batch buttons only ever act on what you can see.

The grid reports the presence and absence of records. It never judges whether a child is ahead or behind. A child's own **Three-Year View**, on the student's page, stays where it was.

## Groups

Which children can you put together for the same next lesson? **Groups** answers it for the whole class. It reads the same record as Today's **Ready for a next lesson** (who you confirmed or marked mastered on a lesson, and isn't planned for the next one yet), so the two never disagree; Today shows the top five and **See all in Groups ›** opens this page. Groups, Today, and the Class Grid use the same two readiness levels, **Ready** and **Ready, check first**.

- **A card is a group:** a lesson with two or more children ready for it, showing how long each has waited in school days (colored once it passes your Lesson Age setting). Cards are sorted by how many children are ready, then the longest wait.
- Children held by open practice (in a sub-area that requires it) are listed in orange with the reason. Children one lesson behind, ready for the lesson before this one, appear dashed as "could join after …". A child who had the previous lesson but isn't confirmed yet shows a **Confirm** button; one tap moves her to Ready.
- **Plan** opens the scheduling sheet with the ready children already chosen. A card also says when the lesson is already planned for some of them.
- Lessons with only one child ready are folded into one row at the bottom; tap it to open them.
- Narrow by level from the toolbar, or by area with the chips. Tap a card's area and sequence to open its **sequence ladder**: every lesson in the sequence, with each child standing on the next one they need, and ‹ › to step through the area's other sequences.

# Needs Attention

**Needs Attention** is the active follow-up view inside **Lessons & Work**. It separates two kinds of guide responsibility so neither can hide the other:

- **Observe or Decide** — presentations whose per-child follow-up is still open;
- **Check Work** — work that is due, overdue, ready for review, or has gone too long without a meaningful check-in.

When the same learning cycle needs both an observation and a work check, it appears once in each section. The presentation row opens the observation and planning follow-up; the work row opens the actual child work.

## Acting on Needs Attention

Open an item to:

- Schedule a check-in
- Mark work as complete
- Re-present a lesson
- Reschedule for later
- Record an objective observation
- Resolve the follow-up when no further guide action is needed

# Tracks

A **track** is a defined curriculum path — a sequence of lessons and skills a student moves through over time. Tracks help you ensure students progress systematically.

### Setting Up Tracks

When creating a track, you define:

- Track name (e.g., "Math: Counting to Multiplication")
- Steps — The ordered sequence of lessons or milestones
- Which students are enrolled

### Monitoring Progress

The Class Grid's **Children** view and each student's Progress tab show the student's position in their enrolled tracks. You can see who's ahead, who's on pace, and who may need additional support.

## Group Tracks

Group tracks let you apply the same progression template to multiple students at once, while still tracking individual progress.

# Backup & Restore

Protect your data by creating regular backups.

## Creating a Backup

1. Go to **Settings**
2. Tap **Backup & Restore**
3. Tap **Create Backup**
4. Choose where to save the file (Files app, iCloud Drive, or local storage)
5. Optionally set a password to encrypt the backup
6. A confirmation toast appears when the backup is complete

The backup file is a JSON document containing all your students, lessons, presentations, work items, notes, and settings.

## Restoring from a Backup

1. Go to **Settings**
2. Tap **Backup & Restore**
3. Tap **Restore from Backup**
4. Select your backup file
5. Choose restore mode:
   - **Merge** — Adds missing data from the backup while keeping your current data
   - **Replace** — Replaces all current data with the backup contents
6. Wait for the restore to complete (a progress bar shows status)

## iCloud Sync

If you enable CloudKit in Settings, your data automatically syncs across your devices via iCloud. This is separate from manual backups — it happens continuously in the background.

# Tips for Daily Use

## Start Your Day with Today View

Open the app each morning and check the Today view. It shows everything on your plate — scheduled lessons, pending work check-ins, meetings, reminders, and todos. Use the date arrows to look ahead at tomorrow or review yesterday.

## Use the Floating Button Constantly

The Pie Menu and Command Bar are designed for the pace of a real classroom. When you notice something:

- **Long-press** and slide to "Note" to capture an observation in seconds
- **Long-press** and slide to "Practice" to log what students are working on
- **Double-tap** and type a quick command like "Gave Leo botany"

## Let Needs Attention Guide Your Follow-Ups

Don't try to remember every follow-up. Open **Lessons & Work → Needs Attention** to see presentations that still need observation or a decision and work that is due for a guide check. Use **Upcoming** and the shared **Agenda** when you are ready to plan ahead.

## Use Check-Ins to Build a Progress Story

Every time you observe a student working, log a quick check-in on their work item. Over time, these build a detailed picture of each student's journey — invaluable for parent conferences and progress reports.

## Review Progress Weekly

Set aside time each week (perhaps Friday afternoon) to open the Class Grid's **Children** view and see where each student stands. This helps you plan the next week's presentations and ensures no student falls behind.

## Back Up Regularly

Create a manual backup at least once a week, or enable auto-backup in Settings. Your classroom data is irreplaceable — protect it.

# Glossary

| Term | What it means |
|------|-------------|
| Presentation | The act of teaching a lesson to a student — the Montessori term for giving a lesson |
| Work | Independent practice or follow-up activity a student does after receiving a presentation |
| Check-In | A teacher's observation recorded while a student is working |
| Track | A defined curriculum progression path — a sequence of lessons or skills |
| Great Lesson | One of the five foundational Montessori cosmic education stories |
| Album | A Montessori teacher's collection of lesson plans, organized by subject |
| Three-Period Lesson | A Montessori teaching method: naming (period 1), recognition (period 2), recall (period 3) |
| Scope | Who a note applies to — all students, one student, or a selected group |
| Pie Menu | The circular quick-action menu that appears when you long-press the floating button |
| Command Bar | The natural-language text input for typing commands in plain English |
| Needs Attention | The Lessons & Work view that combines open presentation follow-ups with work that needs a guide check |
| Mastery | When a student demonstrates proficiency in a lesson or skill |
| Class Grid | One screen, four views (Checklist, Three Years, Children, Recall), showing the whole class against the curriculum |
| Ready, check first | A readiness level: the child is ready for the next lesson, but something from the previous one (open work, a check-in asking for practice, a shaky recall check) deserves a look first |
